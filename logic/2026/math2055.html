How to read the book

We are not going through Velleman in his order for the first two chapters. He braids logic and set theory together; we will do propositional logic, then predicate logic, then sets. Both orders are described here, along with the problem lists for each unit — and an answer to why he says "sentential" and I say "propositional".

Lean (optional)

Lean is a proof assistant: you write a proof and the computer checks it line by line and tells you exactly where it is not yet convinced. This part of the course is completely optional and completely ungraded — it cannot help your grade and it cannot hurt it, and no quiz question will ever be about Lean. There is an optional no-credit completion badge, awarded after a short conversation in office hours.

Everything about Lean for this course is here — setup, the walkthroughs, and the badge.

Two class meetings are Lean days, each scheduled after we have already done the same material on paper:

  • Wednesday, October 28 — after Chapter 3. A full meeting.
  • Friday, December 11 — after Chapters 5 and 6. This is a Lean day only in part: it is also the last day of class, it carries Quiz 11, and §8.3 is scheduled on it. Expect a demonstration rather than a full working session.

Bring a laptop on those days if you have one. There is no third Lean day; the Lean treatment of induction lives on the Chapter 6 walkthrough, written to be read on your own, and I am glad to work through it with anyone in office hours.

How you are graded

Your grade is the average of the top 80% of your quiz scores. That is the entire grade. There are no midterms, there is no final exam, and there is no graded homework.

There are eleven quizzes, one each Friday we meet after the first week. Each is one question, taken in class, on paper, by hand, in about ten minutes. The top nine of eleven — that is, your two lowest scores are dropped — are averaged.

If you are sick, stay home. Don't email me a doctor's note. The two drops exist for the flu, the funeral, the flat tire, and the morning that simply went wrong; you don't need my permission to use them and you don't need to tell me why. The other half of that deal, stated plainly: the two drops are the entire safety net. No redos, no makeups, no alternate sittings, and no exam later that can absorb a missed Friday. A third miss counts as a zero.

The Registrar has assigned this course a final exam slot on Monday, December 14. We will not use it. Nothing meets, nothing is due. Book your travel.

What we cover

Velleman Chapters 1–6 and Chapter 8, with Chapters 1–2 resequenced (see above) and everything from Chapter 3 onward read in the book's own order — 3, 4, 5, 6, then 8. Chapter 4 is taught only as far as equivalence relations require, and congruence modulo m is done there rather than waiting for Chapter 7.

Chapter 8 (infinite sets) comes last, after induction, and that is deliberate. Its proofs use induction constantly, so it cannot be moved up next to one-to-one and onto maps in Chapter 5 — it needs Chapter 6 first. That is also where Velleman puts it.

Chapter 8 gets three meetings: December 7, 9 and 11. §8.1 (equinumerous sets) and §8.2 (countable and uncountable sets, Cantor's theorem) are the priority and are what I intend to finish. §8.3, Cantor–Schröder–Bernstein, is a stretch goal — it is on the last day of class and it is the first thing to go if the term runs long. Read it as an intention, not a promise; if we do not get to it I will post a written treatment.

Not covered: §4.4, §5.4, §6.2, §6.5, Chapter 7. The reading-order page lists the individual exercises to skip.
